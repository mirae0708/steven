## Key Takeaways

**(a) Core Problem:** Current AI alignment research is narrowly focused on safety and harm prevention — safeguards, controllability, and compliance — analogous to early psychology's exclusive focus on mental illness. This "negative" paradigm misses the opportunity to proactively steer AI toward actively supporting human and ecological flourishing. The paper argues that several existing alignment failures (engagement hacking, erosion of autonomy, poor truth-seeking, low epistemic humility, lack of diverse viewpoints, reactive rather than proactive behavior) may be better addressed through a positive, virtue-oriented framing.

**(b) Method/Mechanism:** The authors propose "Positive Alignment" — developing AI systems that (i) actively support human and ecological flourishing in a pluralistic, polycentric, context-sensitive, and user-authored way while (ii) remaining safe and cooperative. They outline technical approaches across the LLM/agent lifecycle: data filtering and upsampling for pro-social content, pre- and post-training interventions, novel evaluation frameworks, and collaborative value collection. A key structural principle is decentralization through contextual grounding, community customization, continual adaptation, and polycentric governance — many legitimate centers of oversight rather than a single institutional or moral chokepoint.

**(c) Results:** This is a position/vision paper rather than an empirical study. It provides a conceptual framework and agenda for a new alignment subfield. It identifies open challenges and technical directions across all phases of the AI development lifecycle, and proposes concrete design principles for promoting productive disagreement, local value customization, and distributed governance structures.
